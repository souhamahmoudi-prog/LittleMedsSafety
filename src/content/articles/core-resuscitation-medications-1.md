## **Core Resuscitation Medications**

**Medications commonly stocked in pediatric code trays for cardiac arrest and other life-threatening emergencies include:**

* Epinephrine: Epinephrine 0.1 mg/mL (1:10,000) is used during pediatric cardiac arrest. Prefilled syringes may facilitate rapid administration during resuscitation.Amiodarone or Lidocaine: Antiarrhythmic agents used for refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) or pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT) after defibrillation and epinephrine.Atropine: Used for symptomatic bradycardia associated with increased vagal tone or primary atrioventricular (AV) block. It is not routinely indicated for all causes of pediatric bradycardia.Sodium Bicarbonate: Routine administration during cardiac arrest is not recommended, as it has not been shown to improve resuscitation outcomes. It may be considered in selected circumstances, including prolonged resuscitation, severe metabolic acidosis, hyperkalemia, or certain toxicologic emergencies.Calcium Chloride: Calcium is not routinely administered during cardiac arrest but may be indicated for specific conditions such as hypocalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypermagnesemia, or calcium-channel blocker toxicity.Magnesium Sulfate: Routine administration during cardiac arrest is not recommended. Magnesium is indicated for torsades de pointes (polymorphic VT associated with a prolonged QT interval) or documented hypomagnesemia.Dextrose: Used to correct documented or suspected significant hypoglycemia during pediatric emergencies. The appropriate dextrose concentration and dose should be selected according to the child's age, weight, and clinical situation.Adenosine: Used for stable, regular narrow-complex supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) and may also be considered for certain regular monomorphic wide-complex tachycardias.Sodium Chloride 0.9%: Commonly stocked for fluid resuscitation, medication dilution or flushing, and management of selected fluid or electrolyte disturbances
